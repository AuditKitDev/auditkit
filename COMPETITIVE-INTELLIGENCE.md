### What Customers Love

- Breadth of integrations (200+)
- Clean, polished onboarding for first-time SOC 2
- Policy templates get you 80% of the way fast
- Good for startups getting their first SOC 2 Type I

### What Customers Hate

- **Price escalation at renewal** -- customers report being locked into costly contracts with limited flexibility
- **Shallow automation** -- surfaces issues but leaves teams drowning in follow-up work; engineers become "part-time compliance managers"
- **Access reviews as paid add-on** -- catches teams off guard
- **Limited scalability** -- works well for first SOC 2, breaks down past 50 employees or multi-region
- **One-size-fits-all support** -- no phone support, self-serve model doesn't work for all
- **Alert fatigue** -- overwhelming minor/false-positive notifications
- **Integration rigidity** -- integrations don't allow much customization
- **Audit quality skepticism** -- Reddit users allege Vanta prioritizes quantity over quality by partnering with auditors who give favorable opinions

### Critical Vulnerability: The 2025 Data Leak

In May-June 2025, a code bug exposed customer data (employee data, MFA settings, tool configurations) to OTHER Vanta customers. Fewer than 4% of customers affected (~400+ companies). Devastating for a compliance company -- this is a reputational goldmine for competitors.

### Enterprise vs Startup Positioning

Vanta was **built for startups** but is aggressively moving upmarket. The tension: enterprise features (risk registers, advanced access controls, multi-entity audits) are bolted on rather than native. Companies that outgrow Vanta's startup-friendly model have limited options within the platform.

## 10. Security Platforms (JupiterOne, Wiz, Orca)

These are **security-first platforms** with compliance features bolted on. They don't compete directly with Vanta/Drata but create overlap.

### JupiterOne

- **Category:** Cyber Asset Attack Surface Management (CAASM)
- **Compliance angle:** Introduced "Compliance 2.0" -- reuse controls across frameworks, evidence mapping for SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR
- **Overlap:** Asset inventory, control mapping, evidence collection
- **Gap:** No policy management, no audit workflow, no trust center

### Wiz

- **Category:** Cloud Security Posture Management (CSPM)
- **Compliance angle:** Covers 26% of SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria technical controls natively. 191 controls tested in 2025 external audit.
- **Overlap:** Cloud configuration monitoring, vulnerability scanning, infrastructure compliance
- **Gap:** Only covers technical controls. No policy, HR, vendor, or organizational controls.

### Orca Security

- **Category:** Cloud-native security (CNAPP)
- **Compliance angle:** Integrates with JupiterOne for compliance mapping
- **Overlap:** Cloud vulnerability scanning, configuration compliance
- **Gap:** Same as Wiz -- technical only

### Competitive Implications for AuditKit

These platforms are **complementary, not competitive**. AuditKit should integrate WITH them (pull security posture data as evidence) rather than compete against them. The compliance tools that win will be the ones that aggregate data from Wiz/Orca/JupiterOne + HR systems + identity providers into a unified audit narrative.

## 14. Customer Switching Patterns

### Why Companies Switch (Ranked by Frequency)

1. **Price escalation at renewal** -- the #1 trigger. First-year deals at $7-10K ballooning to $20-40K.
2. **Outgrowing the tool** -- startup tools (Vanta, Sprinto) break down at 50-200 employees. Features like risk registers, multi-entity audits, advanced RBAC aren't there.
3. **Adding frameworks** -- companies that started with SOC 2 and need ISO 27001 + HIPAA find their current tool lacks depth in new frameworks.
4. **Poor support at scale** -- self-serve support works for startups; growing companies need dedicated CSMs.
5. **Integration limitations** -- as tech stacks grow, compliance tools that can't integrate with niche tools create manual work.
6. **Audit quality concerns** -- companies that got a "checkbox" SOC 2 realize they need deeper security, not just a report.
7. **Consolidation play** -- larger companies want to consolidate GRC tools rather than run 3-5 point solutions.

### What Makes Tools Sticky

1. **Historical evidence** -- once you have 2+ years of audit evidence in a platform, migrating is painful
2. **Policy library** -- customized policies are deeply embedded in workflows
3. **Auditor relationships** -- if your auditor uses the platform, switching means finding a new auditor
4. **Integration setup** -- re-connecting 50+ integrations is a multi-week project
5. **Team training** -- new tool = new learning curve for the whole team

### Switching Windows

- **Annual renewal** -- the primary switching moment. 30-60 days before renewal is when companies evaluate alternatives.
- **Post-audit** -- after completing a SOC 2 Type 2, companies reassess if the tool was worth it.
- **Headcount milestones** -- crossing 50, 100, 200 employees triggers re-evaluation.
- **New framework needs** -- adding ISO 27001 or HIPAA is a natural evaluation point.

## 15. Gaps in the Market

### What ALL These Tools Get Wrong

#### 1. Evidence Collection is Still Mostly Manual
Manual evidence collection takes 20-40 hours per assessment. Even API-based tools fall short due to permission/data format issues. **Nobody has solved evidence collection from non-standard, legacy, or custom systems.**

#### 2. Post-Audit is Abandoned
Every tool focuses on getting you TO the audit. After the audit, you're on your own for 11 months until the next one. Continuous compliance is marketed but not delivered -- most tools just run periodic checks rather than providing true continuous assurance.

#### 3. The "Compliance Engineer" Problem
Engineers become part-time compliance managers. Tools surface 200 issues but don't prioritize or auto-remediate. **The tools tell you what's wrong but don't fix it.** Auto-remediation is the holy grail nobody has cracked.

#### 4. Multi-Entity / Multi-Framework Complexity
Companies with multiple legal entities, subsidiaries, or products in different compliance regimes get zero help. Every tool assumes one company = one compliance program.

#### 5. Audit Trail Integrity
Compliance platforms store your evidence, but who audits the auditor? **Nobody provides tamper-evident, cryptographically verifiable audit trails.** This is AuditKit's existing strength (hash chains, Merkle proofs).

#### 6. Developer Experience is Terrible
Compliance tools are built for compliance teams, not developers. No CLI, no CI/CD integration, no policy-as-code, no infrastructure-as-compliance. DevSecOps is lip service.

#### 7. Pricing Transparency
See Section 13. Every tool hides pricing behind a sales call. The company that publishes honest, transparent pricing wins mindshare.

#### 8. AI Compliance (ISO 42001, EU AI Act)
Only Secureframe and Scytale have early support. This is greenfield and growing fast.

#### 9. Small Business Accessibility
The cheapest tools are still $7,000/yr. For a 5-person startup, that's a meaningful expense. **There's no "Stripe of compliance" -- simple, self-serve, pay-as-you-go.** The open source options (CISO Assistant, Probo) are filling this gap but lack polish.

#### 10. Third-Party Risk is Fragmented
Only 56% of orgs use TPRM technology. Only 33% use risk-weighted methods. Vendor risk is still spreadsheets for most companies. The compliance tools that include TPRM are shallow.

### White Space Nobody is Building

1. **Compliance-as-Code** -- policies, controls, and evidence mapped to infrastructure code. Think Terraform for compliance.
2. **Auto-remediation engine** -- don't just flag issues, fix them. Auto-rotate credentials, adjust IAM policies, update configurations.
3. **Evidence marketplace** -- reusable, verified evidence packages that transfer between audit periods and frameworks.
4. **Micro-compliance** -- per-feature or per-API compliance checks embedded in CI/CD. Ship a feature, get compliance checked automatically.
5. **Tamper-proof audit trails** -- cryptographically verifiable evidence chains. AuditKit already has this.
6. **Compliance for AI systems** -- ISO 42001, EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF. Early movers will own this category.
7. **Pay-per-framework, monthly billing** -- no annual contracts, no sales calls.

4. **Developer-first** -- CLI tools, CI/CD integration, policy-as-code. Win the engineers, win the company.
5. **Open source core** -- Consider open-sourcing non-differentiating components to build community and compete with CISO Assistant.
